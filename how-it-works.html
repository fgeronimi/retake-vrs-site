How it works

Valve ranks Counter-Strike 2 rosters with its Regional Standings (VRS) to invite teams to Major qualifiers. This site republishes Valve's standings, explains them, and checks that every published value can be recomputed.

What is ranked

A roster is a group of players, not an organisation. Matches are read from the most recent backwards; a lineup belongs to the first roster it shares three players with. Prize money follows the lineup that actually played the event.

The starting value

Over the last six months, each roster gets four factors between 0 and 1:

  • Bounty offered: its own prize money, best ten events, weighted by age.
  • Bounty collected: the prize money of the teams it beat, best ten wins.
  • Opponent network: how many distinct teams its beaten opponents themselves beat.
  • LAN wins: its best ten wins on LAN.

Each is compared with the 5th best roster, so a top team scores 1. Wins at high-stakes events count more. The average of the four factors is mapped onto 400 to 2,000 points.

Head-to-head

All matches are then replayed in order with an Elo-like update: beating a stronger team earns more than beating a weaker one. The final value is the starting value plus these adjustments. Older results weigh less, and results from the last month count in full.

What retake-vrs checks

For every roster, the starting value, the head-to-head sum, the factors and the age of every match are recomputed from the published pages. Valve rounds what it publishes, so a value counts as recomputed when the gap stays within what that rounding allows. A ✓ on a roster sheet means exactly that.

The model itself was ported to Python and checked against Valve's own code: identical rosters and ordering, and ratings equal to within 10-12 point.
