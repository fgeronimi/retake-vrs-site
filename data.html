    Where the data comes from, and where it goes

    Before you see a team's points on this site, the numbers go on a long trip. Here it is, step by step, like a recipe.

    1. 1

      Teams play matches

      All over the world, teams play matches in tournaments. Some on stage in front of a crowd, some online from home.

      The grown-up nameCounter-Strike 2 events

    2. 2

      Someone writes them down

      Websites write down every match: who played, who won, and how much prize money each team took home.

      The grown-up nameHLTV for Valve, Liquipedia for OpenVRS

    3. 3

      We copy it into a safe box

      We copy those lists into a safe box. We never write on the copy, so we can always go back and check.

      The grown-up nameBronze · Google Cloud Storage

    4. 4

      We sort and check

      We put each piece in the right drawer and check that nothing is missing. If something looks wrong, everything stops.

      The grown-up nameSilver · BigQuery tables and quality checks

    5. 5

      We do the maths

      We use Valve's own recipe to give each team points. Then the computer plays the next tournaments thousands of times to see who could be invited.

      The grown-up nameGold · the model in Python and the simulation

    6. 6

      A robot builds the website

      Twice a day, a little robot wakes up, looks for new matches and rebuilds every page, in English and in French.

      The grown-up nameCloud Run job at 06:00 and 18:00 UTC · GitHub Pages

    From a lot to a few

    Lots of matches go in. A handful of teams come out with a ticket to the Major. The bars are drawn to scale.

    Old matches count less

    A match from last week counts fully. A match from five months ago counts only a little. After six months, it does not count at all: it is like it fell out of the window.
